Single, EP or album: how to size a promotion budget

The size of the release should decide the size of the plan. A first single and a label album need different things, and spreading a small budget across five channels rarely works. Start with what you release, then pick the channels.

Many teams sell bundles built around this idea. The music promotion packages for independent artists on Artist Push, for example, go from one-platform starters to release packages that combine streaming, YouTube ads, blogs and radio. The steps below work whether you buy a bundle or plan each channel yourself.

A first single

Pick one platform where your listeners already are. The goal is a profile that does not look empty when a new listener lands on it. Plays, saves and a few followers on one platform do more than thin results on four. Keep the rest of the budget for the next release.

A single you want to push

Add a second channel to the first one. A short YouTube ad campaign and one music blog feature work well together, because a reader can click straight to the video. Order the blog early, since a real person has to listen and schedule the post.

An EP or a strong single

Here a few days of steady activity matter more than one big day. Combine streaming with YouTube ads and two or three blog features. Spread them across the first week so each channel points listeners to the others.

An album or a label release

A larger release can cover streaming, YouTube ads, blogs and radio at the same time. Playlist pitching and a 14-day radio rotation need lead time, so start them before release day. Write down the budget for each channel before you buy anything, and keep notes on what worked for the next album.
