How internet radio promotion works for an independent release

Internet radio is one of the few channels where a new track plays next to other music in its genre without an algorithm deciding first. A station adds the song to its rotation, and listeners hear it between other tracks. For an independent artist, that also gives something to show press and venues: links to real stations where the song is playing.

At Artist Push we run internet radio promotion packages on US-based stations that stream 24/7. The notes below describe how such a campaign usually works, so you know what to ask any radio promoter.

What you send

A radio campaign needs a finished audio file, not a link to a stream. Prepare an MP3 or WAV, the artist name, the genre and the release date. Check the master before you send it. A station plays the file you give it, so a weak mix stays weak on air.

How rotation works

The station adds the track to an active public playlist. During the campaign the song plays at a steady rate, for example once every 15 to 20 tracks. A typical campaign runs for 14 days. That is long enough for regular listeners to hear the song more than once.

How to check that it plays

Ask for direct links to the stations and playlists. You should be able to open a station in a radio app or directory and hear the track yourself. Stations that are listed in public radio directories are easier to verify than private streams. If a promoter cannot give you links, treat the campaign with care.

What radio does and does not do

Radio gives exposure and something to point to. It does not promise a jump in streams, and no honest promoter can guarantee one. Some artists see more Shazams and a slow lift in streams in the second week. Plan radio as one part of a release, next to streaming, blogs and video.
